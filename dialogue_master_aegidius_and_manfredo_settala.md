# **Dialogue of Master Aegidius and Hermit Manfredo Settala**

**Characters:**

* **Master Aegidius** — Whose scholastic distinctions are a liturgy of the logical intellect, treating reality as an architecture of causes that can be unlocked by reason.
* **Manfredo Settala** — Whose silence is a cartography of the void, treating the world as an unheeding mass that crushes human ambition into dust.

**Setting:**
*October 1235 — Monte San Giorgio, above the hamlet of Albio. The air is thick with the sediment of long-held spiritual exhaustion. An outcrop of Triassic limestone bearing the fossilized, contorted spine of an ancient marine reptile rests near the threshold, its ridges caught in the fading mountain mist—the “Silent Third Presence.”*

---

## **I. The Weight of the Horizon**

**Master Aegidius:**
(He watches a drop of rain roll down his leather-bound manuscript, his voice straining against the mountain chill)
They say that when I debate in Paris, I am trying to build a new architecture of truth. But maybe, Manfredo, I am just trying to make the terrifying chaos of creation less unbearable to look at. If the mind sees itself as a reflection of the Divine Intellect in the logic, perhaps it will begin to act like it in the world. You think my distinctions are a cage; I think they are a compass that has been polished by necessity.

**Manfredo Settala:**
(He watches his own calloused hands, pale and thin against the rough stone)
A polished compass points only to what it is meant to see, Aegidius. You offer them a map of their own omnipotence, which is a mercy, I suppose. But I have always preferred the rock that is fractured. The people don't just need to see themselves as masters of reason; they need to know that someone else has felt the cold that makes them shake at night. My silence is not a hoarding of grief; it is a confession. Is it not more honest to tell the truth about how it feels to be abandoned by the cosmos, than to tell the lie of how it feels to map the mind of God?

---

## **II. The Anatomy of the Myth**

**Master Aegidius:**
(He runs a thumb over the damp texture of the stone wall)
Perhaps we are both just trying to negotiate with the same silence. You choose the hair-shirt, and I choose the syllogism. But tell me—when the parchment finally rots, when the ink bleeds into the gray mud, will your silence be any more durable than my logic? The *noia* of the universe does not discriminate between the university disputation and the hermit's cell. It swallows both.

**Manfredo Settala:**
(His gaze shifts to the fossilized spine)
Of course it does. Look at it, master. Even its agony is permanent. It suffered once, and the mountain remembered. We suffer every day, and the world forgets before nightfall. You spent your life fearing that your texts would be forgotten; I spent mine fearing that they would be answered. We were both chasing the same ghost—the idea that our lives had a shape the world would be forced to recognize. But look at that bone. It doesn't care about the university or the divine plan. It is just matter. Our "intellect" was the game we played to keep from realizing that we were just part of the furniture of the dark.

---

## **III. The Arid Truth of the Interlude**

**Master Aegidius:**
(He turns from the wall, his eyes searching the hermit's with a rare, quiet vulnerability)
I thought the syllogism could hold the world together.

**Manfredo Settala:**
(He reaches out, his hand touching the scholar's trembling wrist with a deep, unshakeable tenderness—a brief, grounding friction)
It held you together, master. That is enough. But the world was never yours to bind. Your logic is a lantern, Aegidius. But the mountain is older than light.

*(Master Aegidius falls entirely silent, his breath catching in his throat as the absolute weight of the truth settles over him.)*

**Manfredo Settala:**
The irony is that your grand logic gave people hope, and my empty wilderness gave them a place to hide. We weren't masters of the cosmos. We were just the ones who chose to name the stone before it turned back to dust.

---

## **IV. The Silence of the Mountain**

**Master Aegidius:**
(He lowers his gaze, his fingers loosening their grip on the fabric of his sleeve)
Perhaps the silence was the truth all along.

**Manfredo Settala:**
(He closes his eyes, a faint, tired smile on his lips)
Rest, then. You carried the weight of heaven for them. Let the mountain carry you now.

(Aegidius sits in the shadows, his presence less a doctor of the church and more a tired man. The cell is quiet, save for the rhythmic, indifferent dripping of water. The fossilized spine remains, a white, skeletal anchor in the center of the threshold. Outside, the world—vast, unconcerned, and absolute—continues its slow, mechanical turning, unburdened by the names the two men tried to carve into its skin. The silence is not a tragedy. It is the only thing that never lied to us.)
