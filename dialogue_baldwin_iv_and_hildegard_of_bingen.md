# **Dialogue of Baldwin IV and Hildegard of Bingen on the Walls of Jerusalem**

**Characters:**

* **Hildegard of Bingen** — A visionary abbess of the Rhine, seeing the universe as a vast, living tapestry of *viriditas*. She speaks with the luminous authority of one anchored in celestial order and divine purpose.
* **Baldwin IV** — The Leper King of Jerusalem, worn by decay and physical torment. He speaks with the blunt realism of the flesh, carrying the bitter weight of *Il Vero*.

**Setting:**

*The windward terrace of a ruined fortress overlooking the Judean desert at twilight. The sun drops like a dying ember behind distant, chalk-white hills, painting the dry limestone in shades of cold violet. The air smells of dust, ancient salt, and dried thyme. A solitary, centuries-old olive tree—the “Silent Third Presence”—stands between them, its trunk split and hollowed by lightning, its silver leaves rustling dryly in the high air without bending to the wind. A distant vulture circles the valley below, indifferent to the crowns or prayers of men.*

---

## **I. The Illusion Introduced**

**Hildegard:**

Look upon the expanse, my Lord. Is it not suffused with the *viriditas*—the living, greening breath of the Divine Fire? The universe is no chaotic void, but a vast, harmonious wheel, crafted like a precious jewel. Each star, each creature, each suffering spirit is bound into the cosmic fabric. You carry a heavy mantle upon this earth, royal child, but do not doubt that your trials are weighed in the celestial balance. The Living Light reveals to me that even the agonizing weight of your crown is ordained to refine the soul, to echo the sacred architecture of the heavens.

*([Moment of False Hope]*

*Baldwin turns his linen-bound face slightly toward the horizon. For a fleeting instant, as a solitary shaft of gold pierces the violet dusk and strikes the golden rim of his crown, his heart leaps—he feels a sudden, warm swell of purpose, imagining that the aching heat in his bones is indeed the sacred forge of a transcendent destiny.)*

**Baldwin:**

You speak of wheels and celestial harmonies, Abbess, with the breath of one whose lungs are whole. But I feel only the grit of the dust under my sandals and the warmth of the sun fading from stone. My hand inside this velvet glove does not bleed holy incense; it turns to yellow tallow. What has your Living Light to say to the skin that peels away like dry birch bark when I unlace my mantle?

---

## **II. The Dissection of Meaning**

**Hildegard:**

The flesh is but the vessel, King of Jerusalem! The outer shell must wither so that the inner flame may burn with unblemished radiance. Suffering is the chisel of the Divine Craftsman, shaping mortal pride into eternal splendor. The kingdom you hold is not of stone and mortar, but a testament to the order of the Word. Even in your affliction, you are an instrument of a cosmic choir that spans from the seraphim down to the humble root of the field.

**Baldwin:**

A choir? If there is a song, Mother, it is sung in a language that leaves the lips numb. I have led armies with arms bound in linen to keep the flesh from sloughing off the bone during the charge. I sacrificed my youth, my blood, and the comfort of human touch to preserve a city of stone because I believed the heavens watched. Yet when the wind blows across this terrace, it does not distinguish between the scent of my royal ointments and the rot of a dead mule in the valley below.

*([Sensory Indifference Detail]*

*As Baldwin speaks of his agonizing sacrifice, a small lizard scurries across the hot flagstones, stops over a flake of dried leather fallen from the King's sleeve, flicks its tongue, and vanishes into a crack in the masonry without a sound.)*

---

## **III. The Arid Truth**

**Baldwin:**

*(His voice grows low, dry as scraping slate)*

You sell a comforting fable, Abbess. You call nature a loving mother, but she is *la natura matrigna*—a cold stepmother who breeds us only to consume us. What is your divine vision but *l’inganno*—the pleasant error we wrap around our shoulders to keep from shivering in the dark?

Look at this body. The universe did not design my leprosy to teach my soul humility; it is merely matter collapsing into matter, governed by the same brainless rot that turns fallen timber into mold. Where you see sacred order, there is only *la noia*—the endless, indifferent grinding of the world, cycling from dirt to pain and back to dirt. *Il patimento* of the flesh is not a key to salvation; it is simply the price of having senses to feel the cold. The stars do not turn for Jerusalem. All of us are bound together in *l’infelicità comune*. Beyond the sun lies only *il nulla*.

**Hildegard:**

*(Standing firm, her gaze unflinching, her tone taking on the iron clarity of an abbess who has ruled monasteries and defied emperors)*

And yet—you sit here wearing the gold.

If all is *il nulla*, Sire—if the heavens are hollow and nature is a stepmother who does not care whether your city stands or burns—why did you bind your rotting arms in linen? Why did you ride out to Montgisard in the blinding heat? If the song is a lie, why do you continue to march to its cadence?

---

## **IV. The Final Irony**

**Baldwin:**

*(He stiffens slightly. His gloved hand twitches over his knee, then falls still).*

Because without the song, Mother, the limbs refuse to move at all.

**Hildegard:**

Then it is not empty, Baldwin. You call it *l’inganno*—a delusion. But that very delusion held Jerusalem longer than your cold truth ever could. You say nature created us for nothing; yet out of that nothing, man creates a virtue that nature never possessed. If it is an error, sire, tell me: why do men suffer and die for it?

**Baldwin:**

*(Lowering his eyes to the darkening valley, his voice dropping to a low, fatal whisper)*

Because they cannot live without it.

*(A long, heavy silence descends over the terrace. Neither speaks. The wind dies down completely. Hildegard remains standing, her hand resting on her wooden cross, her faith unbroken yet shadowed by the void he exposed. Baldwin remains seated, a king entombed in velvet and bandages, sustained entirely by a ghost he knows to be false.*

*Between them, the split olive tree catches the last pale ray of twilight, standing silent, motionless, and uncaring in the gathering dusk.)*
