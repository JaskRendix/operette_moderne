# **Dialogue of Robespierre and Sade in the Cell of the Bastille**

**Characters:**

* **Maximilien Robespierre** — The Incorruptible, champion of civic virtue and architect of the Cult of the Supreme Being. He speaks with the razor-sharp, austere fervor of a lawyer-priest who believes society requires a moral cosmic order to survive.
* **Marquis de Sade** — The Prisoner of Vincennes and the Bastille, philosopher of absolute transgression. He speaks with the dark, aristocratic, sensuous cynicism of a man who sees Nature purely as an engine of destruction, pleasure, and decay.

**Setting:**

*A damp, subterranean cell in the Bastille, July 1789. Outside, the distant, muffled roar of a mob shaking the city walls sounds like thunder underground. Water seeps steadily down the moss-covered limestone blocks. Between them sits the **Silent Third Presence**: a heavy iron fetter attached to the wall, empty, rusted solid, and caked with dried straw—indifferent to whether the ankles it once bound belonged to a royalist, a philosopher, or a revolutionary.*

---

## **I. The Illusion Introduced**

**ROBESPIERRE**

*(Standing by the heavy oak door, listening to the echoes in the corridor, his linen shirt pristine despite the dust)*

Listen to them, Marquis. That is not mere riot; it is the labor pain of a new world! The people are rising to dismantle the dungeons of feudal darkness. But a revolution built merely on rage will collapse into anarchy. Man cannot live by bread or vengeance alone; he requires a moral axis. We must crown this uprising with the Cult of the Supreme Being—an immortal, divine architecture that guarantees justice, sanctifies civic duty, and assures every citizen that their suffering in the name of virtue is recognized by the heavens!

*([Moment of False Hope]*

*Robespierre looks upward toward the small, iron-barred transom high on the wall. As a single beam of afternoon sunlight cuts through the gloom and illuminates the dust motes around his head, his eyes kindle with a sudden, radiant vision—he imagines a purified republic of brothers, walking hand in hand under the benevolent gaze of an eternal, moral Creator.)*

**SADE**

*(Reclining indolently on a straw pallet, paring his fingernails with a small silver file)*

How exquisitely tedious, my dear Incorruptible. You tear down one altar only to construct another out of paper and sentiment. You speak of a "Supreme Being" who loves justice, yet you stand in a cellar that has stunk of damp rot for four hundred years. Nature is not a benevolent mother holding a moral ledger, Robespierre. She is a ravenous furnace that demands endless combustion. Your grand "Supreme Being" is merely a ghost you conjured to keep yourself from weeping when you look into the abyss.

---

## **II. The Dissection of Meaning**

**ROBESPIERRE**

*(Turning sharply toward the pallet, his voice dropping into a cold, precise edge)*

A ghost? No, Marquis—it is the foundation without which you could not even sit there in your silk vest and write your scandalous pages! You celebrate appetite and crime as if they were freedom, but you are a parasite living off the very order you mock. The libertine always arrives after the builder. You preach that Nature is a lawless beast, yet a society organized according to your principles would not produce free men or philosophers; it would produce corpses and ash. You need our virtue to maintain the walls so you have a quiet corner in which to play the tyrant.

**SADE**

*(Sitting up, his laughter cutting short as his eyes narrow)*

And what are your builders but butcher-architects, Maximilien? You talk of liberty, yet you dream of a republic so pure it can only breathe if it is scrubbed with lye and blood! You want to carve men into neat geometric shapes to fit your immaculate ideal. But Nature does not care for your geometry. She creates through agony; she demands blood to fertilize the soil; she demands murder to clear space for new seed. If your revolution tears this city apart, it won't be in service of your high Supreme Being. It will be obeying the only true law Nature ever wrote: the law of the carnivore.

*([Sensory Indifference Detail]*

*As Sade speaks, a solitary black beetle crawls slowly out of a crack in the damp floorboards, navigates the shadow of Sade’s boot, and disappears beneath the rusted iron fetter without pausing or turning back.)*

---

## **III. The Arid Truth**

**SADE**

*(Standing up, pacing the cell with a panther-like, restless grace)*

Your "Supreme Being" is an *inganno*—a fragile, pathetic error invented by weaklings who cannot bear the beauty of destruction. Where is your moral order in the plague? In the earthquake that swallows an innocent village while the bishop sleeps?

Nature is *la natura matrigna*—a cold, indifferent stepmother who watches a child drown with the exact same serene boredom with which she watches a rose bloom. There is no heaven keeping count of your sacrifices. There is only *il patimento*—the physical reality of muscle, blood, and pain—and the ravenous drive of appetite seeking its fill before *il nulla* closes over us all. All your republican virtue ends in the same quiet dust.

**ROBESPIERRE**

*(Unflinching, his jaw set like iron)*

If Nature is a monster, then it is man’s sacred duty to defy her! We are not beasts driven solely by appetite and rot. Even if the stars are cold, the moral law inside us is real because we choose it!

Your freedom is nothing but slavery to the flesh—a pathetic surrender to the lowest dirt. If humanity is bound in *l’infelicità comune*, then virtue is the only spark that warms the frost! To abandon virtue because Nature is cruel is the act of a coward who kneels before the mud and calls his surrender enlightenment.

---

## **IV. The Final Irony**

**SADE**

*(Stopping inches from Robespierre, his voice dropping to a low, lethal murmur)*

Then go out there and build your temple of virtue, lawyer. But when your words fail and the mob demands flesh, remember that it is Nature who supplies the blade. You will spill rivers of blood to keep your lie alive.

**ROBESPIERRE**

*(Staring straight into Sade's eyes, his voice steady, cold, and absolute)*

Then I prefer blood shed for a principle to blood shed for pleasure.

**SADE**

*(Smiling bitterly as he steps back onto his straw)*

Nature will take both, Maximilien. And she will not mark the difference on the invoice.

*(A loud, thunderous crash echoes from above as the outer gates of the Bastille give way to the crowd. Shouts and gunpowder smoke filter down through the stones. Neither man moves. Robespierre stands rigid by the door, his eyes fixed on the ceiling, bracing himself for the birth of a Republic he will die to enforce. Sade lies still on the straw, resting his head back against the stone, waiting for the chaos he knows he cannot control).*

*(Between them, the rusted iron fetter remains anchored to the weeping wall—empty, cold, and utterly uncaring of the Republic that rises above it or the blood that will follow.)*
