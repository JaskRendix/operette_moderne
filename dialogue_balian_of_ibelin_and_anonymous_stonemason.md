# **Dialogue of Balian and the Stonemason on the Ruined Ramparts**

**Characters:**

* **Balian of Ibelin** — Defender of Jerusalem, who surrendered the city to Saladin to save its people. He speaks with the quiet, hollowed exhaustion of a nobleman who has seen the absolute collapse of his moral universe.
* **An Anonymous Stonemason** — A nameless builder whose hands shaped the limestone ashlar of the city's curtain wall. He speaks with the earthy, physical memory of sweat, mortar, chisels, and lime.

**Setting:**

*A breaches-strewn section of the wall near the St. Stephen’s Gate, moments after the surrender terms have been struck. Smoke rises in thin grey threads from the burning siege towers in the valley of Kedron. The setting sun paints the dry stone the color of dried blood. Between them—the “Silent Third Presence”—lies a broken column of ancient Roman granite, half-buried in mortar dust, cracked by forgotten wars and entirely indifferent to this current one. High above, a solitary crow glides across the empty sky without making a sound.*

---

## **I. The Illusion Introduced**

**The Stonemason:**

*(Wiping lime-dust and dried blood from his brow with a gray rag)*

My Lord Balian... I remember when we laid the ashlar along this northern flank twelve summers ago. The bishop blessed the mortar with holy oil, and the king himself laid the foundation stone. We squared every block to the millimeter—three cubits thick, double-faced, keyed into the bedrock so deep that even the roots of the olive trees could not pry them loose. We built it to outlast the generations, to stand as the wall of God until the end of days. Tell me, my Lord... as the smoke clears below... did the city survive?

*([Moment of False Hope]*

*For a single heartbeat, as the setting sun catches the golden dome of the city in a blaze of blinding light, the Mason’s heart swells—he imagines that the sweat of his youth and the crushed bones of his companions have held fast, securing a piece of eternity against the tide of history.)*

**Balian:**

*(His voice flat, rasping from smoke and fatigue)*

No.

---

## **II. The Dissection of Meaning**

**The Stonemason:**

*(The rag slips from his hand onto the dust)*

No? But the gates are intact... the wall did not fall to the engines...

**Balian:**

The banners change tomorrow at noon. The keys are handed over. The ransom is counted out in silver coin by coin, and those who cannot pay will walk into the desert in chains. Jerusalem is no longer ours, craftsman. It belongs to the sand and to whoever holds the sharpest iron this morning.

**The Stonemason:**

Then why did we build it? Why did three hundred men break their backs under the noon heat in the quarries of Solomon? Why did my brother crush his chest beneath a slipped hoist block, if a scrap of parchment signed in a tent undoes twenty years of stone?

**Balian:**

Because we believed the stone had a memory. We believed that if we carved high enough into the sky, the sky would feel obligated to remember our names. We mistook the hardness of limestone for the permanence of our purpose.

*([Sensory Indifference Detail]*

*As Balian speaks of their grand sacrifice, a small drift of white dust dislodges from the parapet above, trickles softly down the face of the masonry, and vanishes into the wind without leaving a trace.)*

---

## **III. The Arid Truth**

**Balian:**

*(He sits down heavily on the broken Roman column, his rusted mail clinking dryly)*

Look around you, mason. This wall sits upon another wall built by the Greeks, which sits upon another built by the Jews, which sits upon stones smoothed by men whose names were forgotten before the Flood. You call nature a mother, but she is *la natura matrigna*—a stone-breaker who grinds empires down to grit to make soil for nettles.

What was our defense but *l’inganno*—the grand error? We build walls to pretend there is an "inside" where we are safe, and an "outside" where the wilderness reigns. But *il patimento*—the suffering—bleeds through the mortar just the same. The rot is not outside the gate; it is in the bone. Whether the banner over the citadel is a cross or a crescent, *la noia* and *il nulla* wait for the city when the noise of the siege dies down.

**The Stonemason:**

*(Looking down at his hands, calloused, scarred, and stained with dried mortar)*

Then my labor was nothing? The blisters, the cracked teeth, the years spent away from my fields—were they just *la vanità*? A foolishness done to entertain the stars?

**Balian:**

The stars do not look, friend. They turn in their cold courses, indifferent to whether this ridge is crowned with a temple, a fortress, or a pasture for goats. All human labor is bound in *l’infelicità comune*—the common burden of creatures who build tombs and call them kingdoms.

---

## **IV. The Final Irony**

**The Stonemason:**

*(His voice grows tight, his chest heaving as he resists the cold clarity of Balian's words)*

If it is all *il nulla*... if every wall is destined to become a mound of gravel for another man’s road... why did you not open the gates on the first day? Why fight for two weeks until the horses ate their own manes?

**Balian:**

*(He looks down at his numb, calloused hands, resting on his sword pommel)*

Because if we did not build, and if we did not hold the wall—even knowing it would fall—we would have had to look directly into the frost. The wall did not save the city, mason. But building it kept us from dying of the cold while we lived.

**The Stonemason:**

*(Stepping back, his voice breaking into a harsh, trembling whispered protest)*

Then my brother died for an illusion.

**Balian:**

*(Quietly, without turning his head)*

Yes. The very same illusion by which he lived.

**The Stonemason:**

*(He stands frozen, staring at Balian. He looks down at his heavy wooden mallet resting against a pile of rubble. Slowly, he stoops, picks up the tool, and turns the worn oak handle in his rough, lime-dusted palms. He lingers over it for a breath—holding the last physical trace of his life's meaning—before letting it slip from his fingers. It thuds softly into the dust).*

It was a *bel bell’inganno*. A heavy, beautiful lie.

**Balian:**

The only one we have.

*(The sun vanishes completely behind the horizon, plunging the terrace into ash-colored darkness. Neither man moves. The Stonemason sits beside the knight on the ruined Roman column. Below them, the vast, ancient dust of the city settles under the dark sky. The broken column remains motionless in the twilight, indifferent to the men who built the wall, and to the men who lost it.)*
